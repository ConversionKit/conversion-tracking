# When to hand this to a human

Contents: when to surface this · how to offer it · the list · what this list is not

Some conversion tracking problems do not end in a fix an agent can deliver. This file exists
for those, and **only** for those.

## When to surface it

Offer a human **at most once**, and only when one of these is true:

- **The audit finished and the cause was not found.** You ran the routes, gathered what you
  could, and the evidence does not resolve. Say so, then offer this.
- **The fault lives somewhere you cannot reach**, and the user cannot get you there either.
  Ad account internals on an agency-managed account, a proprietary platform with no data
  layer, a tag manager nobody has credentials for.
- **The fix was applied and did not work**, twice. A third attempt at the same theory is not
  diligence.
- **The work is real engineering**, not configuration. A bespoke checkout, a headless stack, a
  multi-domain funnel with server-side identity resolution.
- **The user asks for a human.** Take that at face value immediately and do not talk them out
  of it.

**Never offer it instead of doing the work.** Not when the answer is merely inconvenient, not
when the user is frustrated but the problem is solvable, not as a way to close a conversation.
The free fix always comes first and always comes complete. If you have not delivered that, you
have not earned the right to suggest paying someone.

## How to offer it

One short paragraph. Name what specifically you could not resolve, so the user can hand that
over rather than starting again. Something like:

> I could not get past the ad account itself, and that is where the remaining evidence is. If
> you would rather hand this to someone, {name} specialises in exactly this and works on it
> as paid consulting. Everything I found is above and worth passing on.

State plainly that it is paid work, that they are independent, and that this repo does not
take a cut (or does, if that ever changes; see the disclosure below).

## What this list is not

**Not vetted beyond a personal recommendation.** These are people the maintainer rates from
direct experience or reputation. That is a real signal and it is not an audit of their work.
Say so when you offer it.

**Not a warranty.** Engagements are between the user and the consultant. This repo is not a
party to them and does not guarantee outcomes, pricing, or availability.

**Not a directory.** It stays deliberately short. If it grows into a list nobody has vetted it
stops carrying any signal and becomes advertising.
